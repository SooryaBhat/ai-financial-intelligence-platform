# Guide 10: SME/MSME Business Management and Digitalization

> **Scope note.** Sections 1–9 describe **general international good practice**. Section 10 lists **India-specific legal, tax, regulatory, and government-scheme topics** the business owner should check with official sources or a professional. This guide does **not** state India-specific rules, thresholds, schemes, or definitions because none have been verified: `[INDIA-SPECIFIC: SOURCE NEEDED]`. Nothing here is legal, tax, or professional advice.

## 1. Typical small-business challenges

| Area | Common problems |
|---|---|
| Finance | Mixing personal and business money; thin cash reserves; late customer payments; informal credit given; no budget |
| Operations | Single-person dependence; stock counted by memory; no supplier records; manual order handling |
| Data | Paper or spreadsheet records; inconsistent product names/units; late data entry; no backup |
| Management | Decisions based on feel; no regular review; KPIs not defined; growth outpacing systems |
| Compliance | Records incomplete for tax/statutory filing; lack of time or expertise |

## 2. Recordkeeping and data quality

Minimum viable records: every sale, purchase, expense, and payment with date, amount, counterparty, document reference; stock movements; bank reconciliation. Principles: **one source of truth**, entry at the time of the event, unique IDs, consistent categories and units, correction by reversal (not deletion), regular backups. Poor data quality limits every analytic and AI feature (rules DQ-001 to DQ-010).

## 3. Digitalization and integrated records

Benefits of integrating sales, purchasing, inventory, and accounting: fewer duplicate entries, consistent numbers, faster reporting, audit trail, ability to analyse trends. Steps:
1. Map current processes and records.
2. Decide what you must see weekly/monthly (KPIs).
3. Clean and standardise master data (products, units, customers, suppliers, categories).
4. Migrate historical data selectively (e.g., last 24 months) and keep the originals.
5. Pilot in one process, then expand.
6. Reconcile system numbers with bank and physical counts for the first months.
Risks: bad data migrated into a new system; over-complex tools; staff resistance; unexpected costs; dependence on one vendor (check data export).

## 4. Business continuity

Backup data (tested restores), document critical processes, keep contact lists of suppliers/customers/advisors, cross-train staff, insure major risks as appropriate, plan for power/internet outages (offline procedures), define who can access which data and revoke access when staff leave.

## 5. Basic risk management

Use the risk register approach (Guide 7 §6): list risks, score likelihood and impact, assign owners, review quarterly. Typical SME risks: customer concentration, supplier failure, cash shortage, key-person loss, cyber incidents, compliance lapses.

## 6. Financial resilience

Maintain a cash reserve appropriate to volatility (no universal figure; Guide 2 §11), keep credit facilities arranged before need, diversify customers and suppliers, keep costs flexible where possible, monitor cash weekly.

## 7. Choosing meaningful KPIs

Pick 5–8 KPIs tied to decisions: e.g., revenue per period, gross margin, operating cash flow, overdue receivables, stock cover of key items, stockout days, forecast error. Define each precisely (basis, period, formula, owner, source), review on a fixed schedule, and retire KPIs nobody uses. More KPIs than the owner can act on cause noise.

## 8. Digital-tool adoption

Evaluate: fit with real processes, ease of use, cost (including training and support), data export, security, local-language support, offline capability, integration with banking/accounting tools, vendor stability. Start with the problem to solve; pilot; train; measure whether time or errors actually fell.

## 9. Human review of automated decisions

Automated outputs (alerts, forecasts, recommendations) require human judgement before consequential action. The owner should check source records for flagged items, understand data limits, and keep authority over stock, payments, pricing, and filings. Document who approves what. ALLADIN is read-only and recommends rather than acts (AST-004).
